Model settings

How it works. Each player's pre-game average is a prior. As the game goes on, the model blends that prior with how the player is actually going (a gamma–Poisson update), then works out the chance of getting over or staying under the line in the time left. The quarter clock drives how much time is left, so keep it roughly in sync with the broadcast.

Why multi chances aren't just multiplied. Same-game legs move together. If Brisbane dominate, their players rack up the ball, Fremantle's don't, and the Lions win. 20,000 simulated finishes share one "who's on top" factor, so the multi numbers respect that. Duplicate legs (McCluggage 20+ and 23+) are handled properly too.

Fair value is chance × return, roughly what a fair cash out would be. Bookmaker cash outs usually pay less. Cheer list ranks what would add most to your expected return if it happened now.

Where the numbers come from. Each player's expected count blends his 2026 season average (60%) with his last five games (40%), from the official AFL match stats for all 51 Brisbane and Fremantle games this year. How much in-game pace moves the estimate comes from each player's own game-to-game spread. The Lions' win chance starts from Squiggle's betting-market model. The live feed pulls scores, the quarter clock, disposals, goals and time on ground from the AFL's match centre about once a minute.
